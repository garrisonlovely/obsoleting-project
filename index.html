About the Project

A private industrial program of historic scale, organized around one objective its members state plainly.

Objective

Artificial general intelligence, defined by the Project’s best-known member as systems that outperform humans at most economically valuable work. Several members now describe their goal as superintelligence.

Structure

Member organizations operate under separate brands and compete intensely. They share investors, chips, cloud contracts and researchers, and they invest heavily in one another.

Governance

The Project has no headquarters, no chief executive and no charter of its own. Its direction is set by the capital-allocation decisions of its members, and by the trajectory of the technology.

Risk factors

An excerpt from the Project’s annual report.

The Obsoleting Project
Annual Report 2026
Part I

Item 1A. Risk Factors

An investment in the Project’s objective involves a high degree of risk. You should carefully consider the risks described below, together with all of the other information on this site. If any of the following risks actually occurs, our business, the global economy and the human species could be materially and adversely affected.

Risks Related to Our Products

Our products could cause human extinction.

In May 2023, the chief executives of OpenAI, Anthropic and Google DeepMind signed a public statement declaring: “Mitigating the risk of extinction from AI should be a global priority alongside other societal-scale risks such as pandemics and nuclear war.”24

We do not currently know how to control the systems we intend to build.

OpenAI has disclosed that superintelligence “could lead to the disempowerment of humanity or even human extinction,” and that “[c]urrently, we don’t have a solution for steering or controlling a potentially superintelligent AI, and preventing it from going rogue.”25

Our leadership’s own estimates of catastrophic outcomes are material.

The chief executive of Anthropic has said: “I think there’s a 25% chance that things go really, really badly.”26 The founder of xAI has said there is “some chance that it will end humanity,” and put that chance at “10% or 20% or something like that.”27

Society may not be prepared for our products.

“Humanity is about to be handed almost unimaginable power, and it is deeply unclear whether our social, political, and technological systems possess the maturity to wield it.”28

Risks Related to Our Objective

If we succeed, demand for human labor may decline substantially.

See “Outlook.”

Long-term results may differ materially from near-term results.

In 2015, months before co-founding OpenAI, Sam Altman said that AI “will probably, most likely, sort of lead to the end of the world. But in the meantime, there will be great companies created with serious machine learning.”29

Careers & contact

Careers

Member organizations are hiring in research and engineering. In 2025, the chief executive of OpenAI said a fellow member had offered his staff “$100 million signing bonuses.”32

Candidates for all other positions are encouraged to review the Project’s Outlook.

Government relations

The Project does not lobby as a single entity. Members and their executives support political committees directly, including Leading the Future, a super PAC network launched in 2025 with more than $100 million pledged by backers including OpenAI president Greg Brockman.33

Contact

The Project does not maintain a central point of contact.

For investor relations, product questions, safety disclosures, employment inquiries, regulatory correspondence or press requests, please contact the relevant member organization directly.

Editorial note

The biggest companies in history are racing to build a machine that replaces human labor. The only surefire way they won’t succeed is if we stop them.

There is no Obsoleting Project, Inc. It is the journalist Garrison Lovely’s name for that race, from his book Obsolete. This site gives the race the website it would have if it were a single company. Every quotation on it is real, on the record, and sourced in the notes below.

The AI Industry’s Trillion-Dollar Race to Replace Us — and How to Stop It

By Garrison Lovely · OR Books & Nation Books · Out now
